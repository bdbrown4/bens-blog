# Confabulation, Not Hallucination
### A better word for when AI gets it wrong — picked up somewhere on the highway to Indy

## The drive

Last weekend I drove to Indianapolis to visit family for my brother's law school graduation — a chance to celebrate him and let my extended family spend some time with the boys. The boys were in the back seat with Katie, locked into *Toy Story* on the tablet, which meant I had the front of the car and a long stretch of road to myself.

So I kicked on some podcasts and settled into TED AI with Bilawal Sidhu.

## TED AI in my ears

I worked through quite a few episodes. One, hosted by Vox's Julia Longoria, dug into the "Rats" — the Rationalists — and the paperclip maximizer argument. I won't go down that rabbit hole today, but it was *super* interesting and I'll probably come back to it.

The one that really stuck with me, though, was Bilawal's conversation with neuroscientist Anil Seth on AI consciousness.

## A better word than "hallucination"

Seth is brilliant, and he ranged across a lot of ground — from the idea of mediums conveying consciousness all the way to how we tend to anthropomorphize consciousness in the first place. That anthropomorphizing thread is what led into the part I keep thinking about.

We talk about LLMs "hallucinating" when they hit the edge of their context window — context rot, too much information streaming in, and the model starts making things up. Seth pushed back on the word itself. He argued it's unfair to say an LLM hallucinates at all. Hallucination is a very specific phenomenon that *we*, as humans, experience.

A more fitting word, he said, is **confabulation**.

> Confabulation is a memory error where a person — or an LLM, in our case — unconsciously fills memory gaps with fabricated, distorted, or misinterpreted information. It isn't intentional lying; the individual genuinely believes their false recollections or made-up stories are true.

When Seth framed it that way, it clicked. It felt far more accurate — and, honestly, eye-opening.

## What it means to hear each other out

I catch myself trying to map my own experience onto other people's situations, but it's never that simple — for anyone. We each carry our own unique perception of the reality we all share. Maybe the lesson is that we should be a little more thoughtful when we hear each other out.

That's well outside the scope of AI and LLMs, I know. But it feels tangentially related — and it's where my mind went somewhere on the road to Indy.
