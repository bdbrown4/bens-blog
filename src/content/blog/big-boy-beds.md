## 4:30 PM

Last Friday, Katie gave me the green light: the boys' new beds were finally happening. I didn't love the timing — I had a packed day at work and couldn't log off until 4:30 — but the list wasn't going anywhere: tear down two cribs, childproof the room, and build two brand new twin toddler beds before bedtime.

By the time I got off work, Katie was already elbow-deep in it, clearing the room and taking the cribs apart. I jumped in beside her while Archie and Hugh supervised the only way two-year-olds know how — pure chaos. We got both cribs fully disassembled and hauled the pieces outside to deal with later. Demolition, at least, went according to plan.

Then came the part I assumed would be the easy half.

## 6 PM

The beds were IKEA-style flat-pack, from a brand called Ocodile. Genuinely nice beds once they're together — but a note for Ocodile, if anyone there ever reads this: label every part you reference in the instructions. Not most of them. What I got instead was a hodgepodge, some pieces labeled and others left for me to guess at, and untangling which unlabeled bracket was which cost more time than the assembly itself.

I was aiming for an 8 PM bedtime. By the time I'd sorted the parts and actually started building, it was already 6, and I was only halfway through bed one. Waiting behind that was a shelf I still needed to mount, for the camera and their Hatch — a sound machine that plays bedtime stories and brown noise, which I cannot recommend enough.

Katie, meanwhile, was on full boy duty, and I felt it. She's with them all day; usually I get to show up after work and give her some kind of break. Instead, she made dinner, then loaded up the boys for a Lowe's run to grab the last few things for the room — all while still chipping away at her own yard project, which is turning into its own impressive undertaking. I finished bed one right as she was heading out, sprinted downstairs, ate in about five minutes flat, and went back up to face box two.

## 8:15 PM

I took a breath and told myself bed two would go faster now that I understood the Ocodile system. I was half right — it went a little faster, just not enough. I didn't finish until 8:15, already past bedtime, and I still had that shelf to hang, plus a cord protector running up the wall so the boys couldn't yank the cords out of the Hatch or the camera.

I was running on fumes at that point. *Please don't let me do a shoddy job on this last piece,* I remember thinking. Somehow, despite being completely gassed, the shelf and the cord protector went up clean. It looked genuinely good. I felt a disproportionate amount of pride over one small shelf, and I still do.

## 9:30 PM

I scrambled to finish tidying the beds and lock down the shelf hardware while Katie handled bath time solo. Watching her get through it after the day she'd had, I was reminded, not for the first time, that I have no idea how the boys and I got this lucky with her.

By some miracle, the room was done and the boys were ready for bed at 9:30 — an hour and a half past schedule, but done. What Katie and I hadn't really thought through was what came after: this wasn't just a new bed, it was a completely new kind of freedom, and neither boy had ever had it before. That turned out to be the actual project.

## Everything We Tried

From Friday night through the start of this week, bedtime turned into a rotation — one of us in the room, the boys screaming the moment we left, the other tapping in for relief so somebody could get some sleep in their own bed. We tried what we could:

- Blackout film on the window, blinds pushed up out of reach, after the boys took to messing with them out of pure anger
- Books in the room, Katie's idea — it did not help. I've taken a book or two to the face since

By Monday night, the room was about as safe and comfortable as we knew how to make it. There was one more thing left to try.

## Tuesday Night

Katie had one last addition ready to go: "Space Dino" bed covers, basically a sleeping bag stitched right to the mattress, and honestly a great product. We put them on Tuesday, right before we made the call — this was the night we'd let the boys cry it out, as much for our sanity as theirs.

We walked out, closed the door, and braced for it.

Both boys started screaming right away. Archie settled in after about 20 minutes. Hugh held out for 45 before finally throwing in the towel. Katie and I lay there afterward in the silence, both wondering the same thing: was that actually it?

This morning gave us the answer. They slept through the entire night.

I won't pretend crying it out was easy. It's a genuinely hard thing to sit with as a parent — hearing them upset and choosing not to go in. I can't say for certain whether it was finally committing to that approach, the new bed covers, or some combination of both — but whatever it was, it worked. We're sticking with it. Katie and I woke up feeling like ourselves again for the first time in a week.

Wish us luck as we keep figuring out this new bedtime routine with Archie and Hugh. Thanks for reading.
