## The Bulletproof Plan

This past weekend, after work, I made a promise to Katie: I'd pull apart our washing machine and fix the error code it kept throwing.

I had a solid game plan. I'd use Claude to help me diagnose the problem, lay out the steps to take the machine apart, find the fault, fix it, and put it all back together. It was bulletproof.

Until one small mishap.

## Diving In (With Claude in My Pocket)

I dove in with Claude on my phone. I described the error code, then took some pictures of the washer itself. It quickly identified that my drain filter was likely clogged and gave me a clear set of steps to follow.

Unfortunately, on my model — a Whirlpool Duet — getting to that filter means taking apart almost the entire washer. So I wrestled off the top, the front panels, and eventually the whole door too.

With everything off, I used my phone's flash to snap a picture of what I thought was the drain filter, just to verify. Voila — my instincts were right, and Claude confirmed them.

## The Culprit

I started the manual drain, twisting the filter counterclockwise very slowly, and once it was ready, I pulled it out.

What I found... well, it was definitely clogged. A pair of the boys' socks, stuck right smack dab in the filter. I was flabbergasted.

I cleaned everything up, threw away the socks (they were disgusting), and started putting the machine back together. I was moving fast — I wanted to enjoy the rest of my night and actually relax after a long day.

Once it was all back together, I kicked off a quick wash cycle to see it in action. Running smoothly.

## Then My Heart Sank

I couldn't find my phone.

I'd been using it as a flashlight, so my worst thoughts showed up immediately. I frantically searched the entire first floor, hoping I'd just set it down somewhere random. I hadn't.

As soon as the washer finished, I grabbed Katie to help me call my phone. She stood next to me, dialed... and the washer started buzzing.

Dread. Exhaustion. But I knew what I had to do.

I started tearing it all apart again. Thankfully Katie was right there to help me fish it out with our "grabber tool" — I genuinely don't know the technical name for that thing, but it came in clutch.

## The Part I Didn't Need to Do

After the whole debacle, once I was finally sitting on the couch across from Katie, I told Claude what had been clogging the filter.

I knew I didn't have to. But I sometimes get this urge — like the model would otherwise be left with some crisis of never knowing how the problem it walked me through actually resolved. Probably just me projecting my own anthropomorphized experience onto it.

It came back with a response that made me chuckle:

> Called it! 🧦 Twin tax — they take the sleep, the energy, *and* apparently the socks.
>
> Glad you got it sorted. For future reference, cleaning that filter every 6 months takes about 5 minutes and will keep that error from ever coming back. With little ones in the house, maybe every 3.
>
> Now go reassemble and enjoy having a working washer again — something tells me the laundry pile didn't pause while you were doing this.

It may not have been necessary to spend the tokens telling the model it helped me fix the problem. But it made me feel good knowing I did — so maybe it was worth the tokens all along.
